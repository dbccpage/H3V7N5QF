**Does incidence decline? Open.** Before the gates, the logged 10–15 review
has 21 bullets, or 3.5 per reviewed derivation; after the gates, the 17–21
logs have 11 *larger* units, or 2.2 per derivation. Those numbers cannot be
compared as rates because a later unit may bundle several faults (18 and 21
do), the derivations have very different sizes, and review depth differs.
Moreover the current review finds 13 further faults across old and new
derivations. This refutes the simple claim that the gates drove observed
defect incidence to zero. Two witnesses bracket the outcome: the
definitional gate demonstrably caught the three 13–15 rows, whereas the
seventeenth parent-citation omissions persisted after all three gates.
The evidence supports **local feedback** (a failure leads to a detector and
a later detected failure of that shape), but no measured long-run learning
rate. A future test should hold review depth and claim count fixed, classify
each defect into the same units, and compare newly authored derivations
before and after the citation detector.
