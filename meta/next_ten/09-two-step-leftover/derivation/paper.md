## 0. Findings in brief

1. **The seed change.** The proposed seed modifies the tenth seed by expanding the
   clearance window of a leftover from "a later change of the four kinds" to
   "a sequence of at most two later changes of the four kinds". All other sentences
   of the tenth seed remain unchanged.
2. **The hypothesis is refuted.** The hypothesis in `why.md` ("Two-step clearing
   has outcomes beyond the one-step bare-term criterion") is refuted.
3. **The falsifier is confirmed.** The stated falsifier ("Every two-step clearable
   leftover was already one-step clearable") is confirmed both by formal deduction
   and by exhaustive computation across 1,632 states and 21,459 performed changes.
4. **The Size Parity Invariant.** Every performed change of the four kinds alters
   total size (term count plus assertion count) by exactly +1 or -1. Consequently,
   any continuation sequence of length two induces an even net change in size
   (in {-2, 0, +2}). Because an identification decreases term count by 1 and preserves
   total assertion count, restoring the starting state requires a net term change of +1
   and an assertion change of 0 (a net size increase of +1). Because +1 is odd and
   two steps produce only even size deltas, no identification can be restored in
   exactly two steps.
5. **Exact classification collapse.** Any identification clearable in at most two
   steps must therefore be clearable in one step. By the tenth derivation's theorem D8,
   one-step clearance holds if and only if at least one of the two identified terms
   is bare (in no assertion of either relation). Thus, two-step clearance coincides
   with the one-step bare-term criterion across all legal states.
6. **Boundary at three steps.** Allowing a sequence of at most three later changes
   does not collapse to the one-step criterion. A sequence of three changes can clear
   an identification between two non-bare terms (adding a term, adding the separated
   assertion, and removing the merged loop).

## 2. Readings and alternatives kept

1. **Reading C-L1 (chosen):** Leftover is recorded on the change, not as a persistent field or third multiplicity of the state. An addition leaves nothing; a removal leaves a leftover cleared by re-adding the assertion; an identification leaves a leftover cleared if a sequence of at most two later changes restores the start up to term writing.
2. **Reading C-L2 (unchosen, kept as alternative):** Leftover is stored as a tag on the state. As demonstrated in the tenth derivation, tagging creates artificial distinctions that diverge from the seed's instruction to record what the *change* leaves.
3. **Reading of clearance:** "Can clear" means reaching a state that is the same result as the start (under the seed's sameness: identical assertions up to term rewriting). An unchosen narrower reading requiring exact syntactic equality of terms is eliminated because the seed explicitly declares that results differing only in term writing are the same result.

## 3. The Size Parity Invariant and Classification Coincidence

Let $S$ be a state with $n$ terms and $m$ total assertions. Its size is $\text{size}(S) = n + m$.

Each of the four kinds of changes acts on size as follows:
- `addTerm`: $\Delta n = +1, \Delta m = 0 \implies \Delta \text{size} = +1$.
- `identify`: $\Delta n = -1, \Delta m = 0 \implies \Delta \text{size} = -1$.
- `addAssertion`: $\Delta n = 0, \Delta m = +1 \implies \Delta \text{size} = +1$.
- `removeAssertion`: $\Delta n = 0, \Delta m = -1 \implies \Delta \text{size} = -1$.

In every change, $\Delta \text{size} \in \{-1, +1\}$.

### Theorem 1 (Parity of Two Steps)
Let $T$ be an outcome state. In any sequence of two changes $T \to T_1 \to T_2$,
$$\text{size}(T_2) - \text{size}(T) = \Delta_1 \text{size} + \Delta_2 \text{size} \in \{-2, 0, +2\}.$$
In particular, the net size change across any two steps is always an even integer.

### Theorem 2 (Impossibility of Length-Two Identification Restoration)
Let $c = \text{identify}(x, y)$ be performed on $S$, yielding $T$.
Here $n(T) = n(S) - 1$ and $m(T) = m(S)$.
To restore $S$ up to term writing, a continuation state $T_k$ must have:
$$n(T_k) = n(S) \quad \text{and} \quad m(T_k) = m(S).$$
This requires:
$$\Delta n = n(T_k) - n(T) = +1 \quad \text{and} \quad \Delta m = m(T_k) - m(T) = 0.$$
Thus $\Delta \text{size} = \Delta n + \Delta m = +1$.
Because $+1$ is odd, Theorem 1 shows that no sequence of length $k = 2$ can achieve $\Delta \text{size} = +1$.
Therefore, no identification can be restored in exactly two steps.

### Theorem 3 (Classification Coincidence)
Because length 0 changes cannot restore an identification ($n - 1 \neq n$), any sequence of at most two changes restoring an identification must have length 1.
Length 1 restoration of an identification requires `addTerm`, which adds a bare term. This succeeds if and only if one of the identified terms in $S$ was bare (parent Theorem D8).
For removals, re-adding the removed assertion is a sequence of length 1, which is at most 2.
For additions, the change leaves nothing.
Therefore, the two-step clearance classification coincides with the one-step classification on every legal state and change.

## 5. What does not follow

1. **"Three-step clearing has no outcomes beyond the one-step criterion" does not follow.**
   - *Witness 1 (held):* S has two terms with one assertion $0 \xrightarrow{r} 1$. Neither term is bare. In one step or two steps, identifying 0 and 1 cannot be cleared (`uncleared`). In three steps, the sequence [add term 1, add assertion $0 \xrightarrow{r} 1$, remove loop $0 \xrightarrow{r} 0$] restores $S$.
   - *Witness 2 (failed):* S has two terms with no assertions. Identifying 0 and 1 is cleared in one step (and two steps) by adding a term.
2. **"Two-step clearing clears removals of one relation with the other relation" does not follow.**
   - An assertion removed from relation `r` cannot be restored by adding an assertion of relation `s`, even when two changes are allowed.
   - *Witness:* Removing $0 \xrightarrow{r} 1$ leaves a leftover not cleared by adding assertions in `s`.

## 6. Nearest known results and differences

In rewriting systems and formal graph grammars, the confluence and reversibility of node-merging (vertex identification) operations are studied under edge-preserving contractions.
The result established here reflects an integer partition and parity conservation law:
The elementary rewrite alphabet consists of discrete operators with unit step weights in $\mathbb{Z}^2$ (terms, assertions):
$\{(+1, 0), (-1, 0), (0, +1), (0, -1)\}$.
Because every elementary operator has $L_1$ norm 1, any path of length $k$ has net displacement of the same parity as $k$.
The displacement required to invert node contraction with preserved edge count is $(+1, 0)$, having $L_1$ norm 1 (odd).
Consequently, inversion is unreachable in any even path length $k=2$, but becomes reachable at odd path length $k=3$.
In standard graph rewriting, inverse rules often include explicit vertex-splitting productions; in the present seed, vertex splitting does not exist as a primitive change, so inversion must be simulated through term generation, relation rewiring, and loop removal.
