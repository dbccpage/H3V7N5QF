## 2. Local Reading Extraction and Record Sets

A state $s = (V, E)$ with $n = |V|$ terms admits:
1. **0-term reading:** The empty selection $()$. Record: `(0, ())`.
2. **1-term readings:** For each $u \in V$, the selection $(u)$. Record: `(1, ())` in legal states (since loops $xrx$ are forbidden).
3. **2-term readings:** For each ordered pair of distinct terms $(u, v)$ with $u \neq v$, the record is $(2, \text{edges})$, where $\text{edges} \subseteq \{(0, 1), (1, 0)\}$ denotes:
   - `none`: neither $u \to v$ nor $v \to u$,
   - `fwd`: $u \to v$ only,
   - `bwd`: $v \to u$ only,
   - `both`: both $u \to v$ and $v \to u$ (a 2-cycle).

Every state generates exactly $1 + n + n(n - 1) = 1 + n^2$ readings. The set of distinct records observed across these readings forms `record_set(s)`.

## 3. Bounded Exploration and Partition Hierarchy

Starting from the nameless initial state `given(False, Sameness.FREE)` under bound 3 and 12 rounds:
- **Written states reached:** 69 states.
- **FREE canonical classes:** 20 classes.
- **Multiset local reading classes:** 14 classes.
- **Set local reading classes:** 8 classes.

The 8 set equivalence classes partition the 69 states as follows:
1. `{(0, ()), (1, ())}`: 1 state (isolated vertices only).
2. `{(0, ()), (1, ()), (2, ())}`: 2 states (isolated vertices with non-edges).
3. `{(0, ()), (1, ()), (2, ((0, 1),)), (2, ((1, 0),))}`: 10 states (contains directed edges, but no non-edges and no 2-cycles).
4. `{(0, ()), (1, ()), (2, ()), (2, ((0, 1),)), (2, ((1, 0),))}`: 18 states (contains directed edges and non-edges, but no 2-cycles).
5. `{(0, ()), (1, ()), (2, ((0, 1), (1, 0)))}`: 2 states (only 2-cycles, no non-edges).
6. `{(0, ()), (1, ()), (2, ()), (2, ((0, 1), (1, 0)))}`: 6 states (contains 2-cycles and non-edges, no asymmetric edges).
7. `{(0, ()), (1, ()), (2, ((0, 1),)), (2, ((1, 0),)), (2, ((0, 1), (1, 0)))}`: 18 states (contains asymmetric edges and 2-cycles, no non-edges).
8. `{(0, ()), (1, ()), (2, ()), (2, ((0, 1),)), (2, ((1, 0),)), (2, ((0, 1), (1, 0)))}`: 12 states (contains all 2-term configurations).

Total: $1 + 2 + 10 + 18 + 2 + 6 + 18 + 12 = 69$ states.

The strict inequality $69 > 20 > 14 > 8$ shows in this model that local reading sameness is strictly coarser than both written equality and FREE canonical rewriting.

## 4. Colliding Witnesses

### Witness 1: Distinct Written States Collapsing at $n = 2$
Consider:
- $s_{\text{arb}} = \langle 2, \{(0, 1)\} \rangle$ (`arb`),
- $s_{\text{bra}} = \langle 2, \{(1, 0)\} \rangle$ (`bra`).

In the eighteenth derivation, $s_{\text{arb}} \neq s_{\text{bra}}$. However, each state contains one directed edge and one reversed edge under ordered pair readings:
- For $(0, 1)$ in $s_{\text{arb}}$, the record is `fwd`. For $(1, 0)$, the record is `bwd`.
- For $(0, 1)$ in $s_{\text{bra}}$, the record is `bwd`. For $(1, 0)$, the record is `fwd`.
Both yield the identical record set:
$$\{(0, ()), (1, ()), (2, \text{fwd}), (2, \text{bwd})\}.$$
Lean theorem `arb_equiv_bra` establishes `LocalEquiv s_arb s_bra`.

### Witness 2: Non-Isomorphic States Collapsing at $n = 3$
Consider:
- $s_A = \langle 3, \{(0, 1), (1, 0)\} \rangle$: one 2-cycle between terms 0 and 1; term 2 is isolated (2 assertions total).
- $s_B = \langle 3, \{(0, 1), (1, 0), (0, 2), (2, 0)\} \rangle$: two 2-cycles (between 0 and 1, and between 0 and 2); no assertions between 1 and 2 (4 assertions total).

Because $|E(s_A)| = 2 \neq 4 = |E(s_B)|$, $s_A$ and $s_B$ are not isomorphic under any term permutation (`s_A_ne_s_B_size`). However, in both graphs:
- every pair of terms has either a 2-cycle or no assertions.
- neither graph contains any asymmetric directed edges.
Consequently, both yield the identical 4-element record set:
$$\{(0, ()), (1, ()), (2, \text{none}), (2, \text{both})\}.$$
Lean theorem `s_A_equiv_s_B` establishes `LocalEquiv s_A s_B`.

## 5. Non-Congruence of the Step Transition System

In Rule-5 re-checking, when states are quotiented by an equivalence relation, path-dependent claims require checking whether step transitions descend to the quotient:
$$\forall s_1, s_2, s_1', \quad (s_1 \sim s_2 \land s_1 \to s_1') \implies \exists s_2', \quad (s_2 \to s_2' \land s_1' \sim s_2').$$

In the model, this condition fails:
- State $s_A \sim s_B$.
- From $s_A$, removing assertion $(1, 0)$ yields $s_A' = \langle 3, \{(0, 1)\} \rangle$. In $s_A'$, there are no 2-cycles: `rec2 EdgeConfig.both` is absent (`s_A_next_has_no_both`).
- From $s_B$, there are four possible assertion removals: removing $(0, 1)$, $(1, 0)$, $(0, 2)$, or $(2, 0)$. In all four outcomes, the remaining three assertions retain one 2-cycle (`s_B_rem1_has_both` through `rem4`).
- Therefore, no single-removal successor of $s_B$ can have the same record set as $s_A'$ (`s_A_next_not_equiv_s_B_rem1` through `rem4`).

Exhaustive search across all reachable states identifies 31 transition mismatches (`check_step_congruence`). Thus, the step transition system does not descend to the local reading quotient.

## 8. Nearest Known Results and Differences

In graph theory, the question of whether a graph is determined by its induced subgraphs is classical. Kelly and Ulam's *Reconstruction Conjecture* posits that graphs with at least 3 vertices are reconstructible from their deck of $(n-1)$-vertex subgraphs. Here, the seed examines the opposite extreme: *local profiles of subgraphs of size at most 2*.

A 2-term sub-reading inspects only pairwise relations (edges and non-edges). A set of 2-vertex subgraphs can record only which pairwise motifs exist in the graph, losing all higher-order connectivity, degree distributions, and cycle structures. Even when multiset frequencies are retained, pairs of directed graphs with identical degree sequences and edge densities (such as directed paths versus disjoint stars) cannot be distinguished. The findings in this derivation establish that local 2-term reading equivalence is strictly coarser than graph isomorphism, collapsing non-isomorphic graphs while destroying step transition congruence.
